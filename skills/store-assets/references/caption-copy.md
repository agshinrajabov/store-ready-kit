# Caption copy: outcome over feature

Last checked: **2026-10-04** · numbers: 2.3.7 (no prices or rankings in screenshots), 2.3.1 (accuracy)

## The formula

**What the user gets, in their words, in 7 words or fewer.** Not what the app has.

| Feature (weak) | Outcome (strong) |
|---|---|
| Powerful search filters | Find a belayer for tonight |
| Advanced analytics dashboard | See where your money went this month |
| 1000+ levels | (show the most distinctive level; no caption) |
| Easy-to-use habit tracker | Keep the streak you actually care about |
| AI-powered photo editing | Fix the light in one tap |

Tests for each caption:

1. **Would a competitor's screenshot say the same thing?** If yes, rewrite it. Stock phrases ("relax and
   unwind", "train your brain", "all-in-one") are the category talking, not the app.
2. **Can the frame prove it?** The caption describes what the image shows. A promise the frame cannot show is
   a 2.3.1 risk.
3. **Is it readable at search-result size?** Short words, one line where possible, high contrast.
4. **Is it the user's language?** Use the words from `aso-research`'s review mining, not the team's internal
   terms.

## Voice

- Verbs the user does ("Find", "Keep", "Plan"), or the result as a noun phrase. No exclamation marks.
- One idea per caption. No lists.
- Sentence case. Consistent across the set.
- Localise the captions for each market you support, and rewrite them in the user's own words rather than
  translating mechanically.

## Never

Prices, "free", discounts, "#1", "best", awards the app did not win, ratings that are not current, other apps'
names, other platforms (2.3.7, 2.3.10).
